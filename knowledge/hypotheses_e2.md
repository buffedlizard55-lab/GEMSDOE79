## Context — why H33 scored 0.2778 and how to beat 0.3195/0.4413

- **What H33 actually is (raster-measurement, not score proof):** `evidence/h33_forensics.json` shows H33's 37,654 dots are exactly D2.8's 44,090 Poisson 300 m off-catalogue dots **minus 6,436 points within 200 m of mapped faults** (`D2.8 & (dist_to_catalogue >2 px)`). Median catalogue distance 1.97 km, 69.8 % >1 km, 0 adjacent positives. DTI formula: `TP = Σ max_p k(d)`, `FP = Σ p(1-max_k)`, `FN = n_truth - TP`, `DTI = TP/(TP+0.2FP+0.8FN)`, `k(d)=max(1-d/300,0)`. Thinning removes FP mass 0.2*FP term with little TP loss because sparsely spaced dots each cover ~300 m halo without redundant overlap. Pruning catalogue-flank echoes removes FP from already-mapped traces (masked at scoring but penalised via wasted budget that could cover hidden truth). The 0.2778 is an **unverified historical filename-to-score report** (`h33-h33-2-b2-20261004T220000Z-e5eb6e7e-zeros: 0.2778`), not an ORGANIZER-CONFIRMED receipt; public leaderboard now shows 0.4413 (xiaofanhu, 2026-10-21) so 0.3195 is stale.
- **Why beating it is possible but not by naïve emission:** D2.8's Poisson spacing already near-optimal for 300 m kernel. Further gains must come from **spatially informed placement** that puts dots where hidden faults are more likely than uniform, without leaking catalogue labels into features. Geophysical edge-only products historically scored low (0.03–0.09) because same-pixel edge co-location mimics lithologic contacts. Successful extensions must target **structural dilation sites** (stepovers, relay ramps, tips) where permeability and geothermal favorability correlate with fault presence, not just any edge.
- **Official grounding:** Task is fault detection for geothermal (https://www.drivendata.org/competitions/306/competition-doe-gems/page/967/), not vent proof. Known faults masked at scoring (https://community.drivendata.org/t/scoring-clarification-are-known-usgs-ingenious-faults-masked-when-scoring-and-are-they-in-the-final-round-label-set/11516/2). Poisson 300 m kernel pooling clarified (https://community.drivendata.org/t/leaderboard-aggregation-pooled-over-public-test-pixels-or-mean-of-per-chunk-scores/11550/2). GeoDAWN flight 400 m (https://www.usgs.gov/data/geodawn-airborne-magnetic-and-radiometric-surveys-northwestern-great-basin-nevada-and, DOI 10.5066/P93LGLVQ). Reference U-Net baseline (https://github.com/drivendataorg/gems-prize-reference-solution). Rules 3/week cap (https://docs.nlr.gov/docs/fy26osti/96647.pdf). Rank 2-3 also promising but need heavy training_features restore + DEM mosaic. Rank 4-5 lower expected gain.

**Validation plan for top candidate (Rank 1):** Spatially-blocked hide-and-recover via `gems57.holdout.buffered_component_draw` (whole 8-connected components, 3 px visible buffer, 12 px quadrant erosion, seeds 20 & 30), deriving every catalogue feature **only from visible faults** per fold, masking visible faults pixel-exactly, pooled DTI α0.2 β0.8 300 m kernel (gems57-pooled-hide-v2). Leakage canary per feature alone AUC ≤0.90 (max symmetric AUC). Controls: uniform seeded random surface, distance-to-visible inverse (1/(d+100 m)), H33 fold-fair diagnostic. Require paired positive 95 % CI over matched uniform/distance controls on both seeds before promotion. **No weekly slot spent without beating current holdout best.**
