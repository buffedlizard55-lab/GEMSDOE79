## 2. Why that construction scores well — the metric (ORGANIZER-CONFIRMED structure, derivation INFERENCE)

The organizer's distance-tolerant Tversky index with α = 0.2, β = 0.8 and a 300 m triangular kernel
k(d) = max(1 − d/300 m, 0) gives, for a prediction p and hidden truth set G,

TP_w = Σ_g max_{x} p(x) k(d(x,g)),  FP_w = Σ_x p(x)[1 − max_g k(d(x,g))],  FN_w = |G| − TP_w,

DTI = TP_w / (TP_w + 0.2 FP_w + 0.8 FN_w) = T / (0.2 T + 0.2 F + 0.8 |G|) with T = TP_w, F = FP_w.

Four consequences follow, and the champion exploits each one:

1. **Binary dots are optimal.**  A cell's credit and its cost scale together with p(x), so the
   marginal condition for emitting a cell is a ratio test, c/(c+f) > 0.2·DTI, independent of the
   value; there is no benefit to intermediate values, only to choosing *which* cells.  (At DTI ≈ 0.28
   a dot must earn ≈ 0.06 credit per unit of false-positive cost to help.)
2. **Thinning to the kernel scale is free recall.**  Each truth pixel is credited by its *best*
   neighbouring dot, so dots every s px along a true line earn ≈ 1 − s/12 per truth px (s = 3 → ≈ 0.75)
   while the FP mass falls by the thinning factor.  Going from solid ridges (0.1922) to 1.5 px dots
   (0.2477) to 2.8 px dots (0.2600) is this effect, verbatim.
3. **Catalogue pixels are masked, not rewarded** (staff, forum 11516): a dot on or beside a mapped
   fault can only cost.  Removing the 2,545 dots within 2 px of the catalogue raised the score
   (0.2600 → 0.2778), which also shows that the hidden truth is *rare* right next to mapped faults —
   consistent with "new faults" being new geometry rather than re-digitised catalogue lines.
4. **β = 0.8 makes recall worth 4× precision**, so a sparse, well-ranked set of ~38 k dots across the
   whole footprint beats both dense surfaces and tiny high-precision sets.

## 5. Can a *unique* submission beat 0.2778?  (judgement)

The uniqueness rule (≤ 70 % of dots within 3 px of any prior raster's dots, Spearman ≤ 0.90)
forbids another re-dotting.  Arithmetic: keeping the best ≈ 60 % of champion-like dots (≈ 0.125
credit per dot on average, more for the top-ranked ones) and adding ≈ 15 k dots elsewhere is net
positive iff the new dots average ≥ ~0.06 credit — they must be ≈ 25× enriched relative to a random
footprint cell.  Three mechanisms can plausibly deliver that: (a) lidar-visible scarps H19-5 under-
ranks (second-tier steps that are *continuous*), (b) the 24.7 % lidar gap where H19-5 fell back to
quantile-matched 10 m channels, (c) new geometry of known systems (parallel strands, splays), which
the organizer counts.  Geophysics cannot: its live ceiling is 0.10.

**This repository's lane (H79-MCT) is built on exactly that answer**: a terrain-only scarp
likelihood (1 m lidar + 10 m 3DEP + 100 m det_elev, catalogue-free features), gated by the
*line-continuity* of its own evidence (what a mapper's eye does), emitted as 37,654 binary dots
2.8 px apart and > 2 px from the catalogue — the champion's emitter, a different detector, with
≈ 49 % of the dots outside the H19-5 halo (36 % before the duplicate-rule cap), 24 % of them in the lidar gap.
