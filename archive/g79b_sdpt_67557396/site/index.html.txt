<article class="step"><div class="step-no">01 — WHAT H33 ACTUALLY IS</div><h3>44,090 → 37,654 dots</h3><p>Byte forensics: the family's best-reported file is exactly the dotted H19-5 lidar-scarp line set with every dot ≤200 m from the catalogue deleted. It is not a model — it is a metric-optimal <em>emission</em> of one scarp detector. <article class="step"><div class="step-no">02 — WHY IT SCORES</div><h3>Sparse dots, real scarps</h3><p>Under the distance-weighted Tversky metric (α=0.2, β=0.8, 300 m kernel) each truth pixel is credited by its best neighbouring dot, so dots ~3 px apart keep most recall at a fraction of the false-positive cost — and the hidden labels are expert-traced scarps, which a lidar scarp detector matches. Geophysics-only lanes never passed ~0.10 live.</p></article>
<article class="step"><div class="step-no">03 — WHAT G79B PROVED</div><h3>Better ranking, crowded neighbourhood</h3><p>New physics channels (crest–base curvature <em>dipole</em>, antislope polarity, step persistence across scales and two independent DEMs, trace objects) improved the ranking: unconstrained HOLDOUT-DTI 0.1211/0.1114 beats control and archived best on both seeds. But good scarp terrain is exactly where 99 earlier family rasters already put their dots; buying uniqueness there cost 0.018–0.020 — several times the gain. Next lane must be <em>born</em> unique (a detector whose top terrain is not the family's top terrain), not made unique afterwards.</p><a class="text-link" href="https://github.com/buffedlizard55-lab/GEMSDOE79/blob/main/research/hypotheses_g79b.md">G79B hypothesis register (5 ranked hypotheses) ↗</a></article>
