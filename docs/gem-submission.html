Why H33's 0.2778 and the 0.4413 leader are not beaten here

Mechanism reading (PhD-level): H33's reported 0.2778 is consistent with a redundancy-reduction move — D2.8 dotted-ridge emissions minus pixels within ~2 px of the catalogue (no deleted pixel sits exactly on a catalogue pixel). Under the organizer's Tversky-like DTI algebra ``DTI = T / (0.2 T + 0.2 F + 0.8 G)``, truth pixels are covered by the max kernel credit (T saturates per truth cell) while every emitted dot pays a false-positive cost, so removing near-duplicate dots lowers F without materially lowering T, raising DTI. That is a deduplication gain, not a new-fault discovery gain, and H33's exact pixel lineage and any causal leaderboard effect cannot be verified from this checkout (0.2778 is an unverified historical report; the public #1 at 0.4413 is observer-reported, not an organizer receipt for this file).

Can we score higher? Not on anything measured here. G79-GEM's holdout DTI is 0.0045 [0.0029, 0.0064] — below the ~0.05 random control — because this concealed-fault detector targets blind faults absent from the catalogue segments used as holdout truth. Its novelty gate also fails versus a dense prior surface. So there is no measured basis to claim G79-GEM outperforms 0.2778 or 0.4413. Beating them requires a new method that (a) passes the literal novelty gate and (b) clears the incumbent on the buffered holdout; no such method was found in this session, and zero competition slots were spent.
