1. The metric rewards sparse dots, not probability maps

From the official metric definition, with k(d) = max(1 − d/300 m, 0) on the 100 m grid:

TP  = Σ_truth   max_x p(x)·k(d)          (each truth pixel takes its best nearby prediction)
FP  = Σ_x       p(x)·(1 − max_truth k(d)) (every predicted unit pays unless it is on/near truth)
FN  = N_truth − TP
DTI = TP / (TP + 0.2·FP + 0.8·FN)

Known catalogue pixels are masked from scoring (staff clarification), and terms are pooled over the public chunks before the ratio (aggregation clarification).

Two asymmetries follow directly:

  1. TP is a max, FP is a sum. A single dot of value 1 sitting on a straight truth trace credits the pixels at 0, ±100 m and ±200 m with 1, 0.667 and 0.333. Repeating dots every 3 pixels along the trace gives each truth pixel its best kernel value, so each dot earns about 1 + 2×0.667 = 2.33 truth-pixel units of TP while paying at most 1 unit of FP. A continuous map pays FP on every positive pixel and earns no extra TP from neighbours that are already covered. Dotting is the reason almost every high GEMSDOE entry, H33 included, is a binary sparse field.
  2. Break-even probability. Adding a dot with expected TP gain A and FP cost B ≈ 1 improves the score s when A > 0.2·s·(A + B), that is A > 0.2s / (1 − 0.2s). With A ≈ 2.33·P(dot lies on a truth trace): at s = 0.28 a dot is worth placing if P > ≈2.5 %; at s = 0.44 it must be P > ≈4.1 %. (ESTIMATE; it ignores partial-kernel hits and overlap between dots.)

A rigorous lower bound also follows. Write c = TP/N (the share of truth credit captured) and r = FP/N. Then c = s(0.2r + 0.8)/(1 − 0.2s) ≥ 0.8s/(1 − 0.2s), so a public score of 0.2778 needs c ≥ 23.6 % and 0.4413 needs c ≥ 38.4 %.

The precise metric argument

Official formula:

  • k(d) = max(1 - d / 300 m, 0).
  • TP = sum_truth max_prediction[p(x) k(d)].
  • FP = sum_prediction p(x) (1 - max_truth k(d)).
  • FN = number_of_truth_pixels - TP.
  • DTI = TP / (TP + 0.2 FP + 0.8 FN).

The staff clarification excludes exact known-fault pixels, not a 200 m buffer around them. The aggregation clarification requires pooling terms first rather than averaging fold DTI.

A dot can cover several truth pixels. Therefore its marginal true-positive credit A is not generally bounded by its own nearest-truth credit q. For a new unit dot, its false-positive increment is B = 1 - q. If the current score is s, adding it improves DTI precisely when

A > 0.2 * s * (A + B).

Only in the special case A = q does this simplify to q > 0.2*s. We do not use that special-case threshold as a universal claim. Removing a redundant dot with negligible lost coverage but positive false-positive mass can improve DTI; this is why thinning is plausible. But removing a useful dot can hurt recall. Geometry alone cannot determine the hidden-truth trade-off.

The -zeros encoding is a format policy, not a scientific discovery. Replacing outside-footprint NaN with finite zero does not add in-footprint geological evidence. Without the rejected bytes and server receipt, neither NaN nor a sentinel can be proven to have caused the user's earlier range error. Our writer simply rejects nonfinite/out-of-range outputs, never clips them, and checks the serialized result and single-TIFF ZIP.

Why a larger score cannot be inferred from this family table

  1. Confounding: many submissions inherit the same ranking or dot set. They are not independent scientific replications.
  2. Distribution shift: withheld existing faults are not the organizer's new expert-labelled faults. High catalogue recovery may select familiar exposed faults and miss weak, covered structures.
  3. Multiplicity: dozens of ideas and emission settings compared on one proxy can overfit that proxy even when each model is superficially cross-validated.
  4. Measurement limits: a 100 m grid does not imply independent 100 m airborne observations. USGS reports Area 2 flight lines spaced 400 m apart. Fine-scale registration lags can reflect interpolation, levelling or flight geometry.
  5. Novelty gate degeneracy: under the supplied literal support convention, one whole-footprint prior rejects every nonempty candidate. A filename or byte hash cannot resolve this.

What this new lane did, and did not, establish

Four hypotheses were preregistered before implementation. E1 computes opposite-side magnetic-gradient profile registration across an independent gravity-gradient normal. It requires a nonzero lag to beat zero lag and retain its sign at two offsets. This targets marker displacement, unlike the inspected same-pixel cross-gradient and orientation-consensus methods. It is only a hypothesis: an oblique intrusive contact or survey seam can mimic it.

The new numerical surface exists and was exported through the unmodified shared writer. It is byte-distinct from the 992 restored registry files. The literal pre-placement shared gate nevertheless reports 100% support overlap with a verified dense prior. Scientific work stopped before canaries, hide-and-recover or dot placement. The downloadable TIFF is negative-result evidence, not a validated detector. We explicitly do not invent a HOLDOUT-DTI or CI in run_card.json, and do not claim this lane is better or worse scientifically than the incumbent on the official run card.

In a separate post-stop offline diagnostic (evidence/post_stop_diagnostic.json, which does not alter run_card.json or evidence/experiment_state.json), evaluating E1 under hypothetical Decision D-1 Option B (top-37,654 overlap 0.0119 vs E-proba) or Option C (p >= 0.5 overlap 0.3046 vs E-proba) showed that while E1 passes all 72 feature-fold leakage canaries (max_symmetric_auc = 0.5872 on seed 20, 0.6162 on seed 30, both <= 0.90), its pooled HOLDOUT-DTI (gems57-pooled-hide-v2) is 0.0474 [0.0390, 0.0552] on seed 20 and 0.0466 [0.0388, 0.0543] on seed 30—failing to beat its matched zero-lag edge control, underperforming uniform random placement (0.0504 / 0.0496), and trailing the fold-fair H33 control (0.0961 / 0.0989). Likewise, independent canonical audits (evidence/concurrent_lanes_audit.json) of the two archived concurrent lanes (H79-MCT in archive/h79_mct_c26efec/ and H79-CSPT in archive/h79_cspt_3d386a3/) confirm that both failed the literal 0.70 overlap gate (1.0000 overlap against 17GEMSDOE_E-proba-multiscale) and relied on unauthorized local dense-prior exemptions and post-duplicate constrained overlap capping (0.6800 and 0.6900).

Next decision, not another submission gamble

Resolve the protocol/registry ambiguity through an explicit authorization before any new scientific experiment. Then require whole-component buffered hide-and-recover, feature-alone leakage canaries, a matched reproduction of the incumbent, paired spatial confidence intervals and literal novelty checks. The separate selector—not this pipeline—may consider a weekly slot only after those gates pass. A public leaderboard value alone never authorizes promotion.

Additional primary-literature review — implications, not new experiments

Reviewed on 2026-10-21, after the mandatory E1 stop. This review did not change features, consume another experiment, label new faults or authorize a second lane. It provides context for interpreting failures and planning an explicitly authorized future study.

Hermant, Kiersnowski & Bellanger (Stanford Geothermal Workshop, 2025)

Using Deep Learning to Map Quaternary Faults in Western USA — full paper. All eight parsed chunks, including methods, discussion and references, were read. This is a primary author publication hosted by Stanford, not an organizer receipt, USGS endorsement, or evidence that its model was reproduced here.

What is useful for this problem:

  • The paper distinguishes incomplete, regionally heterogeneous public mapping from true fault absence. Its Figure 2 illustrates mapping discrepancies; this supports treating catalogue zeros as unlabelled, not certified negatives.
  • The authors use approximately 10 m elevation/slope and Sentinel-2 B8A imagery, not this lane's 100 m magnetic/gravity stack. They restrict their elevation selection to lidar-derived areas after observing slope artifacts elsewhere (§3.1). Resampling a coarse grid cannot manufacture that independent evidence.
  • Their §5.1 procedure randomly splits fault-containing image tiles before data augmentation. Their paper metrics are PR-AUC and weighted focal IoU, not the competition's pooled distance-weighted Tversky. Neither these metrics nor qualitative figures establish our blocked hide-and-recover performance. A random tile split is not a substitute for our required buffered whole-segment split; this is a protocol distinction, not proof that their results leaked.
  • The authors report overfitting and explicitly identify paleo-shorelines, canyon margins, streams and other scarps as fault mimics (§6–7, Figures 8–9). A long, clear lineament is therefore not sufficient geological evidence. Automatically excluding every shoreline or drainage feature is unsafe too, because faults can be co-located with them.
  • Faults can conduct or impede fluid flow (§2). Better fault detection is not proof of a permeable, hot or commercially productive reservoir. Their expert manual correction is not an automated submission-validation method; it was not substituted for our automatic gates.

Practical consequence: a future, separately authorized morphological lane would need independent data provenance/resolution checks, a non-fault-mimic control, and buffered spatial tests. Proprietary FaultSEG weights and its tracing algorithm are not claimed to be available. This project neither implemented nor scored a CNN from that publication.

Official INGENIOUS project and USGS regional geophysics

  • Great Basin Center for Geothermal Energy — INGENIOUS project describes integrated structural, geophysical, thermal and exploration work; its primary project/publication listing was reviewed. It does not establish independent hidden-test labels or commercial discovery from a raster alone.
  • USGS, Glen et al. (2022), Regional geophysical maps of the Great Basin lists a multi-source provisional gravity grid, a magnetic compilation and a depth-to-basement grid. The release lists a public 6.5 MB grid ZIP and metadata/readme. This checks that a real official release exists; no new binary download or verified correspondence to a particular mirrored band is claimed. Regional compilations and resampled grids should not be read as uniformly sampled, independent 100 m observations.

These sources strengthen the uncertainty and confounder analysis. They do not make the literal novelty conflict disappear, validate E1, or justify an expected leaderboard gain. The four earlier hypotheses remain qualitative priorities, not numerical score projections.
