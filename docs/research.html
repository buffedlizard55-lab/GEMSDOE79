2 / high Repeated channel deflection/knickpoint alignment across independent catchments. Bare-earth elevation/DEM, derived flow direction, channel profiles, catchment boundaries, plus visible-only fault orientation for scoring. Detect same-sense deflection or profile-break alignment where several independent channels cross a common corridor. A low-slip or reworked piedmont fault may perturb drainage after its scarp has degraded; requiring independent catchments makes a single local terrace less persuasive. The repository implements local lidar scarp/curvature and a bajada-knickpoint feature in the DFSD lane, but the source search found no actual flow-routing and multi-catchment alignment pipeline. It is not simply another local curvature or scarp score. Lithologic contact, transient incision, stream capture, or terrace riser. Require cross-catchment replication and lithology-matched randomized corridors. Requires source DEM tiles rather than only the current 19-band stack. Specific free official source: USGS 3DEP / The National Map. Obtainability is not verified here: tile coverage, download access, and study-area completeness have not been checked, so this is not currently viable.
3 / high Conjugate-strike accommodation from vector strain rotation. Required inputs: raw geodetic tensor/vector components (if available), rtp (2), iso_grav_anom / iso_grav_anom_hg (13/18); fault orientations recomputed from visible catalogue only. Target a spatial rotation in principal strain direction across a shear transition co-located with a potential-field boundary. Short oblique R/R′ or transfer splays may be omitted where maps emphasize major range-front traces. Existing lanes model scalar strain magnitudes, tips/relays, and magnetic–gravity junctions; the proposed signal is a tensor-direction rotation, not scalar dilation or a fault-tip cone. Pluton/caldera-ring boundary, regional geodetic smoothing, or joint fabric. Direction-only, potential-field-only, station-geometry, and matched non-fault controls are required. The local feature inventory identifies scalar shear/dilation but does not establish tensor components/directions. DOE INGENIOUS/GDR resource listing is a lead, not proof the required vector product is in the available package. Product schema and obtainability were not verified; therefore not viable unless the exact official tensor source is identified and acquired.
4 / high QA Paired well–spring geochemistry/temperature disequilibrium along a measured hydraulic gradient. Only chemistry, depth, temperature, and location fields actually present in an acquired INGENIOUS table may be used; pair them with competition gravity layers. A reproducible up-gradient/down-gradient residual pattern could identify concealed fluid pathways laterally displaced from mapped traces, rather than merely favoring spring clusters. Prior lanes used thermal/well/spring proximity, geothermometry, and vent proximity; this would test paired chemical disequilibrium and transport direction, not point-density or isotropic proximity. Lateral groundwater outflow, mixing, sampling-depth bias, or clustered sampling. Use leave-cluster-out validation and a spatially randomized point/gradient null. Specific official lead: DOE INGENIOUS GDR 1391. The precise chemistry fields, schema, gradient data, footprint coverage and local bytes are unverified. Because the candidate depends on those fields, do not call it viable before acquiring and auditing the official files.
5 / very high Quality-stratified well heat-flow residuals against an official conductive background, optionally tested for alignment with competition gravity boundaries. Use only actual residual, depth, and quality fields from the source. Local advective heat anomalies may accompany buried permeable structures even where no surface scarp is visible. Signed residuals and well-quality strata could separate local anomalies from the broad conductive background better than a smooth regional map. Existing lanes use shallow temperature proxies and thermal point proximity; no inspected lane uses a source-provided, quality-stratified heat-flow residual with a leave-well-cluster-out test. Non-fault groundwater advection, borehole correction, sparse sampling or background-model error. Require quality-stratified and spatial leave-cluster-out controls. Specific official source lead: USGS ScienceBase heat-flow metadata, DOI 10.5066/P9BZPVUC. Metadata does not confirm the residual field, schema, coverage, license of the needed bytes, or local acquisition. Not viable pending those checks.
