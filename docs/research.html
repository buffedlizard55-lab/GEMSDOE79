$$\text{DTI}(p, G) = \frac{\sum_{g \in G} \max_{|x - g| \le 3} p(x)\,k(|x-g|)}{\text{TP}w + 0.2 \sum{x} p(x)\big[1 - \max_{g \in G} k(|x-g|)\big] + 0.8\,\text{FN}_w}, \qquad k(d) = \max!\left(1 - \frac{d}{3},\, 0\right)$$

  1. $\ell_\infty$ Kernel Saturation vs $\ell_1$ False-Positive Accumulation (d = 2.8 px Poisson-Disk Thinning): TP_w evaluates a pointwise maximum over all predictions within $R = 3\text{ px}$ ($300\text{ m}$), whereas FP_w sums linearly over every predicted pixel. A dense continuous strip of width $W = 6\text{ px}$ along an uncertain fault segment multiplies false-positive cost by $\sim 5\text{--}12\times$ while leaving TP_w nearly unchanged once a single crest pixel hits $p(x)=1$. Poisson-disk thinning at $d_{\min} = 2.8\text{ px}$ eliminated redundant adjacent mass (~75% FP_w reduction) while preserving $\bar{k}_{\text{line}} \approx 0.72\text{--}0.79$ along continuous traces (0.2600 -> 0.2708, UNVERIFIED HISTORICAL REPORT).
