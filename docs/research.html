1 G79-CJX: cross-scale contact-junction localization. Extract gravity and magnetic ridge directors; detect persistent orientation winding/branch points across smoothing scales, then require compatible signed cross-contact contrasts in both fields. Use locally phase-randomized fields as a nuisance null, not as synthetic fault labels. Pinned training_features.tif: band 2 rtp and band 13 iso_grav_anom, checked from the cached band tags. A buried intersection or termination may retain density/susceptibility contrasts without an obvious surface scarp. This is a hypothesis, not evidence that every junction is a fault. E1 measures cross-field lag, THVC uses a learned multi-feature ensemble and visible-catalogue endpoint cones. Neither implements a persistence-qualified director-winding junction statistic against a matched field-texture null. Pluton margins, volcanic caldera contacts and survey-block seams also produce junctions. Compare with closed-contact controls; reject an effect confined to seams or a single scale. Moderate but very uncertain potential; moderate implementation cost. Input bytes restored and hash-verified locally. Nominated only for a future authorized lane; preflight blocks execution now.
2 G79-BLC: bilateral lithology-contrast change-point. Along a data-derived potential-field ridge, compare multichannel distributions on opposite flanks; look for a persistent, abrupt along-strike change in cross-flank ordering rather than high edge magnitude alone. Same pinned feature stack plus external/geodawn_rad_u8.tif and external/geodawn_extensions_u8.tif. Use radiometric ordinal ranks only; no calibrated concentration interpretation. A transfer fault could offset two pre-existing lithologic contacts under alluvium even where the transfer trace itself has weak relief. Not the E1 across-normal maximum lag or a scalar K/Th anomaly. The proposed statistic is an along-contact change in bilateral joint distributions, with flanks defined without catalogue linework. Depositional facies changes, lava-flow boundaries and drainage redistribution can create the same contrast. Require independent gravity support and leave-one-survey-block replication. Low to moderate, uncertain potential; moderate/high cost because local backgrounds and ridge tracking need validation. Cached inputs are obtainable; radiometric units and censoring remain limitations.
3 G79-RSS: relief-screened structural saddle. Locate multi-scale detrended-elevation saddles connecting adjacent basins, then require a gravity contrast crossing the saddle rather than following a closed topographic contour. Pinned training_features.tif: band 12 det_elev, band 19 det_elev_slope, band 13 iso_grav_anom and band 18 iso_grav_anom_hg. No routing on detrended elevation as though it were a true hydrologic DEM. A low-relief transfer zone between range segments may be omitted when maps emphasize long range-front scarps. Existing tip/relay channels start from visible fault endpoints; this proposal starts from catalogue-free terrain topology and tests independent geophysical contrast. It is not an extension of stopped relay allocations. Stream capture, erosional wind gaps, landslides and resistant-bedrock saddles. A terrain-only ablation and independent geophysical test must distinguish these. Low/uncertain incremental value; moderate cost. Cached data are available; coarse relief does not resolve a narrow surface fault.
4 G79-HFR: paired hydrothermal recharge/upflow residuals. Use the published well heat-flow residuals, not the smoothed conductive background, to test spatially paired warm/cool departures aligned with an independently inferred structural corridor. USGS Heat flow maps and supporting data for the Great Basin, DOI 10.5066/P9BZPVUC, specifically the well-point residual attribute and quality fields; cached gravity/magnetic features for structural context. Hydrothermal advection can expose buried permeability pathways without mapped scarps. The official release explicitly identifies residuals as potentially informative about hydrothermal or groundwater influence. THVC/FNHR use nearby temperatures, spring points and geothermal context; this proposal separates positive and negative departures from the source's conductive-background estimate and tests a paired transport signature. Ordinary groundwater circulation, pumping, incorrect borehole corrections and uneven sampling. Leave entire well clusters out; depth/quality fields must be available before modeling. Scientifically promising but unquantified; high acquisition/QA cost. Official metadata and ZIP listing are accessible; binary contents/coverage were not acquired. NOT data-ready / NOT currently viable for validation.

A prior top hypothesis is not data-supported as written

The old H80-MT-PHASE plan calls for resistivity slices at shallow depths, including 500 m and 1500 m. The linked official release, USGS Electrical Conductance Maps, instead provides depth-integrated conductance. Its layer named surface spans 2–12 km, and the remaining ranges extend deeper. Conductance cannot simply be relabelled as shallow resistivity or a smectite clay cap. The former plan's broad coverage and blind-system percentage claims are not established by that source. Do not implement that plan from these products without a different, verified source and resolution analysis. The original text is retained as historical material, not active scientific guidance.
