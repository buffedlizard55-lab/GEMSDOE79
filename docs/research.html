H1 — Damage-zone distance decay from catalogue faults (G79-DZD) — IMPLEMENTED

Layers: USGS/INGENIOUS existing_faults catalogue (mapped Quaternary faults) only; competition footprint mask from sample_submission.tif.

Physical signature: Fault damage-zone permeability — fracture-enhanced permeability concentrates in the fractured rock adjacent to fault cores, not on the mapped traces themselves. Geothermal fluids exploit damage zones where fault-core sealing is bypassed. A Gaussian damage-zone score centred at an optimal ~200 m cross-fault distance, decaying along strike, targets conduits absent from the catalogue because they lie off mapped traces but within the damage-zone envelope.

Why it catches catalogue-missing faults: Catalogue traces are the fault cores. Unmapped splay faults, relay ramps and damage-zone fractures offset from the trace produce geothermal permeability at a distance from the mapped line. Distance-decay scoring finds them without needing to map each trace.

Differs from repo: G79-THVC uses an 84-channel multi-physics ML ensemble (gravity, magnetics, shallow temperature, sinter, LiDAR scarps) with a kernel cover allocator. G79-DZD is a single deterministic distance transform — no ML, no multi-physics stack, no training data needed.

Expected DTI improvement: Modest standalone (damage zones are narrow). Best as a prior or sparse seed in combination. Estimated holdout-DTI 0.08–0.14.

Implementation cost: Low (~1 hour; implemented as scripts/generate_unique_submission.py).

H2 — Shallow-temperature residual + radiometric potassium anomaly conjunction

Layers: DOE GDR submission 1391 (DOI: 10.15121/1881483) 2-m shallow temperature probe residuals; GEODAWN airborne radiometric potassium (%K) from the USGS northwestern Great Basin survey.

Physical signature: Blind geothermal systems leak heat to shallow groundwater, producing positive shallow-temperature residuals above background. Potassium enrichment in altered volcanic rocks (argillic/advanced argillic alteration) co-locates with hydrothermal conduits. The conjunction (both anomalies present) is specific: neither temperature residuals nor potassium anomalies alone are diagnostic, but together they target blind hydrothermal plumes with no surface fault expression.

Why it catches catalogue-missing faults: Blind geothermal systems may have no surface fault scarp at all — heat and chemistry reach the surface through diffuse flow or young faults not yet mapped. Temperature + K+ conjunction finds them directly.

Differs from repo: No lane in this repo combines GDR shallow-temperature residuals with GEODAWN radiometrics as a conjunction. G79-THVC used temperature as one channel among 84 in an ML ensemble — not a two-variable conjunction prior.

Expected DTI improvement: High if blind systems dominate missing faults. Estimated holdout-DTI 0.12–0.18.

H3 — Quaternary volcanic vent + sinter/travertine proximity with step-over dilation

Layers: GDR volcanic vent points (gdr_volcanic_vents_in_footprint.csv, already in data manifest); USGS/INGENIOUS catalogue; derived step-over/dilation zones from catalogue fault geometry.

Physical signature: Volcanic vents are magmatic heat sources; sinter/travertine deposits mark fossil hot springs. Step-overs and dilational jogs between en-echelon fault segments create localized dilation (volume increase) that focuses geothermal upflow. Proximity to vents + sinter + dilation zones targets active geothermal plumbing.

Why it catches catalogue-missing faults: Dilational step-overs are often sub-resolution at 100 m and may not be individually mapped. Vent/sinter proximity captures the heat source and surface expression without needing the exact fault trace.

Differs from repo: G79-THVC mentioned "volcanic vent conduits" as one channel among 84. No lane builds a dedicated vent+sinter+dilation proximity prior with step-over geometry.

H4 — Moment tensor / focal mechanism consistency with catalogue strike

Layers: USGS/INGENIOUS catalogue fault strikes; regional earthquake focal mechanisms (USGS ANSS / ComCat, free); derived fault-slip tendency.

Physical signature: Active geothermal systems correlate with faults that are optimally oriented for slip in the contemporary stress field. Faults whose strike/dip match the regional σ1–σ3 orientation (high slip tendency) are more likely to be permeable conduits. Mismatched faults are sealed or inactive.

Why it catches catalogue-missing faults: Catalogue includes many mapped Quaternary faults of varying activity. Slip-tendency filtering identifies the subset most likely to host active geothermal flow, effectively down-weighting sealed fossil faults that dominate the catalogue.

Differs from repo: No lane in this repo uses focal mechanisms or slip tendency. All existing lanes use static geophysical/geomorphic proxies.

Expected DTI improvement: Medium. Estimated holdout-DTI 0.08–0.14.

Implementation cost: Low-medium. USGS ComCat API is free and scriptable.

External data required: USGS ANSS/ComCat focal mechanisms (free, public, https://earthquake.usgs.gov/data/comcat/).

H5 — 3DEP LiDAR curvature + TPI anomaly in the 24.99% LiDAR coverage gap
