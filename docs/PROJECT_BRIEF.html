Ranked hypotheses — all untested here

Expected DTI improvement is unknown; ranks are qualitative priors, not score claims. No registered hypothesis here has passed validation.

1. Cross-sensor structural edge co-location

Layers: magnetic anomaly (RTP/TMI), conductivity or conductive-base depth, radiometric channels/ratios if present. Signature: scale-consistent gradients with orientation agreement and co-located/offset boundaries across independent sensors. Potential: fault juxtaposition or fluid alteration may affect multiple physical fields. Mimics: lithologic contacts and survey seams. Cost: medium; highest qualitative prior, no uplift quantified. Must inspect band metadata and feature-alone leakage. If radiometric bands are missing, the official [USGS GeoDAWN release](https://doi.org/10.5066/P93LGLVQ) lists GeoTIFF products and CC0 rights; landing metadata was accessed, but no grid file was downloaded and grid compatibility remains unverified.

2. Strain tensor discontinuity

Layers: dilatation, shear-strain rate, second invariant, optionally magnetic orientation. Signature: persistent cross-scale boundaries rather than raw high strain amplitude. Potential: deformation discontinuity could mark an unmapped structure. Mimics: regional strain gradient/interpolation artifacts. Cost: medium-high; medium-low qualitative prior.

3. Paired magnetic edge / offset horizon

Layers: TMI/RTP, horizontal/vertical derivatives, magnetic source-depth estimate if present. Signature: plausible paired edges, offsets, or terminations rather than isolated magnetic ridges. Potential: structural discontinuity in source boundary. Mimics: lithologic boundary, flight-line residual, source-depth change. Cost: medium; medium-low prior and substantial overlap with prior magnetic/Euler work.

4. Seismic-density edge corroboration

Layers: earthquake density plus geophysical/strain orientation. Signature: seismicity gradient coincident with an independent structural boundary. Potential: seismic response can be independent evidence for a missing fault. Mimics: detection/catalogue completeness variation or a non-fault swarm. Cost: low-medium; low qualitative prior and overlap with earlier tectonic candidates.
