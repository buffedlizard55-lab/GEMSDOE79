03 / WHY 0.2778 LED, AND CAN IT BE BEATEN

The leaderboard is distance-weighted recall.

With alpha 0.2 and beta 0.8 the index is T / (0.2T + 0.2F + 0.8G). When false positives are small it is almost monotone in recall r = T/G:

DTI ≈ r / (0.2r + 0.8)  ⟹  r ≈ 0.8·DTI / (1 − 0.2·DTI)

The 0.26–0.28 cluster removes prediction mass within two pixels of the mapped catalogue while keeping a sparse dot field elsewhere. On this metric that is a false-positive saving, because the organizer masks mapped faults from scoring — a dot beside a known trace but far from a new fault is fully penalised. Whether a copy of a known trace is redundant or useful is decided by the hidden labels, which is why the 0.2778 figure cannot be attributed to the thinning alone.

Beating 0.4413 requires covering more of the hidden population at the same false-positive cost. This session measured why the project has not: catalogue-proximity channels score AUC 0.72–0.79 on hidden catalogue faults and 0.43–0.55 on SGMC faults the catalogue misses; terrain roughness reverses that (0.53–0.68 vs 0.75–0.79). Every archived lane, and this one, mixes in the first family — which is also why 18 registry rasters already contain this candidate's dots.
