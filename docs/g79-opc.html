Hypothesis, mechanism, mimic

Faults occur as populations with anisotropic second-order structure (relay steps along strike, sub-parallel splays at characteristic spacing across strike); the strike-rotated inter-component pair-correlation of the visible catalogue predicts unmapped companion faults.

Normal-fault populations in the Basin and Range are mechanically interacting: spacing scales with mechanical-layer thickness (Soliva et al. 2006) and geothermal-hosting step-overs, terminations and intersections are clusters of closely spaced, overlapping strands (Faulds & Hinz 2015). Catalogue traces are simplified, so companions of a mapped fault sit at learnable along/across-strike offsets that an oriented second-order kernel can transfer to unmapped ground.

Named non-fault mimic: Pleistocene Lake Lahontan wave-cut shorelines and fluvial terrace risers (parallel, regularly spaced linear steps); roads (TIGER distance feature)
