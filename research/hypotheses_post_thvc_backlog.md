## Candidate specifications and data gates

### 1. H80-RANGEFRONT-RELAY — first to preregister only if the novelty issue is resolved

- **Required layers:** the competition-provided `training_features.tif` topographic bands (including the 100 m elevation context), the visible-only fault segments for each hide-and-recover fold, and public USGS 3DEP DEM/point-cloud coverage where it is actually available. Do not assume one-meter coverage across the full footprint; record the coverage mask and use the same coverage rule for controls.
- **Signature:** along a range-front coordinate, detect a segment where relief/footwall morphology and drainage organization continue while the fold-visible catalogue trace ends, then test only a narrow, preregistered basinward relay zone. The score surface must be built from visible faults and non-fault topographic context; no held-out fault geometry may enter features, quotas, or tuning.
- **Mimic controls:** alluvial-fan headcuts, stream incision, resistant lithologic boundaries, and landslide/sackung scarps. A hard-negative guard should require a structural/geophysical cue and test the mechanism separately in blind basins and on mapped controls.
- **Why it is different:** this tests relief continuity across a network gap, not distance-to-catalogue, local scarp polarity, or forward distance from a fault endpoint. The distinction must be confirmed against all archived lane methods before preregistration.

### 2. H80-DRAPE-DECONV — conditional on official line metadata

- **Required layers:** official USGS GeoDAWN magnetic grids, flight-line CSVs, flight-path/survey-outline products, contractor report and metadata; USGS 3DEP terrain for clearance comparison; the competition reference grid. Confirm the flight-height/clearance fields and their units before implementation. If the official files do not expose the needed fields, stop and revise the hypothesis rather than invent them.
- **Signature:** an edge remains coherent across survey lines after line-direction artifacts and altitude/clearance effects are accounted for. Validate by withholding complete spatial fault segments, not random pixels.
- **Mimic controls:** tie-line leveling, micro-leveling, flight-line artifacts, pluton/lithology boundaries, and lava-flow margins. A line-parallel edge alone is a negative control, not evidence of a fault.
- **Why it is different:** earlier lanes used gridded magnetic features; this proposal explicitly tests the survey acquisition geometry. GeoDAWN's nominal flight design is not an exact per-pixel clearance model.

### 3. H80-RADIO-KD — conditional on channel metadata and spatial coverage

- **Required layers:** official GeoDAWN radiometric products and their field dictionary/units; the public USGS ASTER-derived alteration map; competition topography/visible-fault context. Only calculate a K/eTh-style residual if the released field names, units, and calibration confirm that operation. Otherwise test a named, documented alteration class without inventing a ratio.
- **Signature:** an alteration residual forms a line-like, structurally aligned pattern rather than a broad basin or isolated point anomaly, and it adds information beyond existing geophysical/thermal channels under spatially blocked holdout.
- **Mimic controls:** playa evaporites, bedrock units, tailings, vegetation/soil effects, and broad weathering zones. Hold out whole basins/ranges when testing whether the signal generalizes.
- **Why it is different:** the novelty is the cross-fitted lithology-conditioned residual and explicit alteration-mimic control, not merely adding another raw radiometric band.

### 4. H80-MT-CONDUCTANCE — revised scope; do not revive “phase tensor” without a different source

- **Required layers:** the shallowest official USGS conductance GeoTIFF (2–12 km integrated depth) and metadata; competition gravity/topography; fold-visible mapped faults. The linked USGS data release identifies this as integrated conductance derived from a 3D MT model. It is not a set of shallow phase-tensor slices.
- **Signature:** a broad conductance boundary co-locates with an independently supported basin/basement structural edge; treat it as coarse context only and test at a scale justified by the actual raster resolution.
- **Mimic controls:** saline brines, smectite-rich sediment, graphitic/metal-rich lithologies, and basin fill. A broad conductance low/high alone is not a fault prediction.
- **Why it is different:** the repo used geothermal and geophysical features but the reviewed card/input receipts do not show this USGS depth-integrated MT raster. Before any test, verify exact file bytes, grid/resolution, and footprint coverage.

### 5. H80-COULOMB-CFS — exploratory, highest modeling and leakage risk

- **Required layers:** official GDR 1391 geodetic shear/dilation data and/or the linked USGS slip/dilation dataset's stress/geometry fields, plus fold-visible fault traces and a documented elastic/stress parameter set. Do not ingest precomputed values for fault segments hidden in the current fold.
- **Signature:** prospective relay geometry is supported by a visible-only stress perturbation while stress shadows suppress otherwise similar flank candidates. Test the positive and negative components separately against the same controls.
- **Mimic controls:** boundary effects, uncertain segment orientation/termination, and stress fields driven by data outside the visible fold. Repeat over preregistered parameter ranges; reject if sign or location is unstable.
- **Why it is different:** existing crack-tip channels extrapolate locally from visible endpoints; this is a fold-specific finite-segment stress calculation with a falsifiable shadow component. It should be last unless the data and leakage audit become unusually strong.

## Official, obtainable source references and what is actually verified

- [DrivenData task and format page](https://www.drivendata.org/competitions/306/competition-doe-gems/page/967/) — source of the competition-provided grid/feature context and metric. Its raw training stack is absent from this checkout; no training bytes were restored during this review.
- [DOE Geothermal Data Repository, INGENIOUS compilation 1391](https://gdr.openei.org/submissions/1391) — lists MT conductance, geodetic shear/dilation, heat-flow, thermal, fault, and other resources. The five-file local receipt does not include an MT raster; a listing is not proof the raster bytes are present locally.
- [USGS Electrical Conductance Maps of the Great Basin](https://doi.org/10.5066/P9TWT2LU) — public GeoTIFF release, five integrated depth ranges from 2–12 km to 90–200 km; not the shallow resistivity/phase-tensor product originally asserted.
- [USGS GeoDAWN release](https://doi.org/10.5066/P93LGLVQ) and [survey description](https://www.usgs.gov/data/geodawn-airborne-magnetic-and-radiometric-surveys-northwestern-great-basin-nevada-and) — official magnetic/radiometric grids, line data, flight-path products, and survey metadata/report. Actual variable terrain clearance is an interpretation concern; available per-sample fields must be checked before use.
- [USGS ASTER-derived hydrothermal-alteration map](https://doi.org/10.5066/F7CR5RK7) — a thematic map derived from visible/NIR/SWIR ASTER data over the southwestern United States; it is not raw ASTER reflectance, a fault inventory, or confirmation of geothermal production.
- [USGS 3DEP products and access](https://www.usgs.gov/3d-elevation-program/about-3dep-products-services) — free public elevation products at multiple resolutions; the relevant resolution/coverage must be checked for each AOI.
- [USGS slip- and dilation-tendency data release](https://doi.org/10.5066/P9YL58W6) — public shapefiles/KMZ with fault geometry and stress data for catalogue-derived segments; it is not a validated hidden-fault target layer.
