## How H33 achieved 0.2778 (best verified family score) and what must beat it

USER-REPORTED pair 0.2600→0.2778 is consistent with removing near-catalogue FP mass, but no organizer receipt links those numbers to those bytes, and only exact catalogue pixels (not the 200 m collar) are masked in scoring. H33's median dot is 19.6 px (~1.96 km) from the catalogue; 69.8% are >1 km away. It is a broad field, not a halo.

The organizer's metric is `k(d)=max(1−d/300 m,0)` with `TP = Σ_truth max_x p·k(d)` (max over neighbours) and `FP = Σ_x p·(1−max_truth k)` (sum) — DTI = TP / (TP + 0.2·FP + 0.8·FN), pooled over chunks (metric § and clarifications in `knowledge/official_sources.json`). Sparse dotted emission wins because one correctly placed dot credits ≈2.33 truth pixels (k=1, 0.667, 0.333 at 0,100,200 m along strike) while a continuous map pays FP on every positive pixel. H79-NHR's measured HOLDOUT-DTI 0.1431/0.1522 beat its H33 control 0.0961/0.0989 on both seeds, but its dots fell inside 26 earlier dot fields (worst 0.913 vs 0.610 chance) — duplicate → stop.

Beating 0.2778 (and the public leader 0.4413 observed on the leaderboard page) therefore requires **better ranking of unmapped truth**, not denser emission or format tricks. The current blocker is not detector skill alone but **crowding**: every good near-fault detector's top terrain is already dotted by the family.
