## Source checks and interpretation limits

- DrivenData's official [problem description](https://www.drivendata.org/competitions/306/competition-doe-gems/page/967/) states the target is geological faults, says expert-identified faults omitted from the public USGS set form the initial test labels, and names detrended elevation/slope, gravity, magnetics and earthquake-density feature families. It does **not** establish that any proposed transform predicts hidden faults.
- The USGS [GeoDAWN release](https://www.usgs.gov/data/geodawn-airborne-magnetic-and-radiometric-surveys-northwestern-great-basin-nevada-and) states that magnetic and radiometric surveys, lidar, and flight-line products were collected; it cautions that actual terrain clearance varies. Source metadata does not authenticate the missing locally cached derivatives or resolve the repository's conflicting radiometric tags.
- The USGS [Great Basin heat-flow release](https://www.sciencebase.gov/catalog/item/6297d2fad34ec53d276c5b28) was opened in this review. Its metadata describes iterative weighted regression to estimate background conductive heat flow, a well-point residual attribute, and a publicly listed 124.12 MB ZIP. **Listing is not successful acquisition**; no claim about usable coverage, license, or field schema is made.
- The USGS [Quaternary Fault and Fold Database page](https://www.usgs.gov/programs/earthquake-hazards/faults) is an official place to review catalogue scope. Do not infer from a catalogue omission alone that a candidate structure is a real fault or a geothermal conduit.
