## Candidate specifications

### 1. Depth-persistent MT–gravity boundary (rank 1; not tested)

- **Inputs:** the exact georeferenced GDR 1391 magnetotelluric resistivity/phase product at multiple depths, plus the competition `training_features.tif` isostatic-gravity and depth-to-basement channels. `cond_surf` is a single competition feature and is **not** a substitute for a depth-resolved MT product.
- **Physical signature:** a lateral electrical-property boundary that persists across depth and is spatially aligned with a basement-depth or gravity-gradient boundary. The transform would measure the depth persistence and normal alignment, not merely high conductivity.
- **Why it could find an uncatalogued fault:** a buried fault conduit or basin-boundary structure may juxtapose electrical and density contrasts below alluvium, with no mapped surface trace. This is a testable hypothesis, not a claim that every conductor is a fault.
- **Named non-fault mimic:** saline basin brine or a conductive lithologic unit. Require a depth-persistent boundary and independent gravity/basement support; a shallow conductor alone is insufficient.
- **Difference from implemented work:** G79-THVC uses `cond_surf` and gravity-derived features separately and has a conductivity interaction feature; the specific depth-resolved MT phase/resistivity cross-gradient is not implemented. ETP used catalogue geometry only.
- **Official source and availability check:** [DOE Geothermal Data Repository, INGENIOUS submission 1391](https://gdr.openei.org/submissions/1391), DOI `10.15121/1881483`. The local `knowledge/official_sources.json` review says the compilation includes MT and that data may be redistributed with attribution. The local `evidence/official_ingenious_receipt.json` lists five files but **no MT volume**; `registry/data_manifest.json` likewise has no MT volume; no MT file exists under `data/raw`. The official page was not freshly fetched this session. Therefore the exact product, coverage, resolution and direct download remain unverified; do not call the data obtainable or run this hypothesis yet.

### 2. Time-series InSAR deformation corridor (rank 2; not tested)

- **Inputs:** geocoded Sentinel-1 line-of-sight time series from NASA/JPL ARIA, with acquisition dates, coherence/uncertainty and incidence geometry. An optical/land-cover mask may be used only to control coherence and seasonal confounds, not as a fault label.
- **Physical signature:** a persistent, spatially coherent displacement gradient across a basin margin or concealed corridor across multiple acquisitions, distinguished from seasonal and transient motion.
- **Why it could find an uncatalogued fault:** active creep or differential strain may cross a sediment-covered trace where a static DEM has no clear scarp. It would target deformation, not the location of a known line.
- **Named non-fault mimic:** groundwater-pumping compaction or seasonal lake/playa motion. Require persistence across independent time windows, coherence support and a tectonically plausible geometry; a single interferogram is not sufficient.
- **Difference from implemented work:** the existing stack includes static geodetic strain bands but no Sentinel-1 phase time series, temporal coherence model or transient/seasonal decomposition.
- **Official source and availability check:** proposed source [NASA/JPL ARIA](https://aria-share.jpl.nasa.gov/). No ARIA record, product, coverage listing, checksum or data file is in this checkout or source ledger. The previous `research/hypotheses_etp.md` claim that availability was “verified” has no attached retrieval receipt. Source identity is supplied for manual review only; current area coverage, licensing and downloadability were **not established**, so this is not viable now.

### 3. Cross-fitted GeoDAWN potassium residual conditional on thorium (rank 3; not tested)

- **Inputs:** the K, Th and, if metadata confirms, U channels in `external/geodawn_rad_u8.tif`; competition footprint and only the visible-fault mask within each holdout fold. The current 8-bit derivative must be treated as a relative feature, not as calibrated concentration.
- **Physical signature:** a spatially cross-fitted residual from robustly predicting K from Th (and only documented covariates), followed by a multiscale line-coherence measure. This asks whether K is unusually high for the local radiometric background instead of using a raw K/Th ratio.
- **Why it could find an uncatalogued fault:** potassium alteration may accompany hydrothermal fluid pathways that are not mapped as Quaternary faults. It is a surface-sensitive indicator and is not expected to work beneath thick cover.
- **Named non-fault mimic:** primary K-rich felsic lithology or a radiometric survey seam. The regression must be spatially cross-fitted; the candidate feature must be tested alone for leakage and checked against survey direction.
- **Difference from implemented work:** G79-THVC already computes a smoothed local K/Th alteration ratio (`rad_k_th_alteration`), and other lanes use raw ratios. The proposed conditional residual is a distinct transform, but it reuses the same signal and is therefore an incremental, not independent-layer hypothesis.
- **Official source and availability check:** [USGS GeoDAWN release](https://www.usgs.gov/data/geodawn-airborne-magnetic-and-radiometric-surveys-northwestern-great-basin-nevada-and). The local official-source ledger says the release includes aeroradiometric products and notes that gridded values do not imply independent 100 m observations. The `ext_geodawn_rad_u8` input is hash-pinned in the project manifest, but `data/raw/external/geodawn_rad_u8.tif` is absent. A pinned owner mirror can be restored with the repository restore tool; this session did not restore it or freshly fetch the USGS page. The data path is conditionally recoverable, not presently materialized.

### 4. Catchment-scale drainage capture and divide asymmetry (rank 4; not tested)

- **Inputs:** a validated 3DEP DEM at its delivered resolution, the competition footprint, and terrain validity masks. Do not derive a fine-resolution stream network from a 100 m raster or treat interpolated cells as new observations.
- **Physical signature:** catchment-scale channel-network asymmetry, elbows, divide migration and paired headwater/knickzone patterns, summarized over multiple neighboring basins rather than a single local scarp or knickpoint.
- **Why it could find an uncatalogued fault:** cumulative tectonic tilting or a concealed step can reorganize drainage over long periods even after a surface scarp is degraded. The inferred lineament would still be a candidate, not proof of a fault.
- **Named non-fault mimic:** differential erosion across resistant lithology or a river base-level change. Reject isolated anomalies that coincide with only one basin boundary; require repeatable geometry across independent catchments and separate lithologic explanations where a suitable map exists.
- **Difference from implemented work:** existing lanes include local `chi_step_max`, `chi_ksn_max`, `chi_playa_raised`, relief and scarp channels. The proposed feature is network topology/capture across whole catchments, which the audit did not find in executable code or run receipts.
- **Official source and availability check:** [USGS 3D Elevation Program](https://www.usgs.gov/3d-elevation-program). The older `official_ingenious_receipt.json` records derived 3DEP topography and chi products from a prior state, but these files and the raw DEM are not present now; the current `data/raw` directory is absent. No fresh USGS download check was made. A suitable tile set and current checksum must be confirmed before this is called viable.

### 5. GeoDAWN flight-line-aware magnetic edge correction (rank 5; not tested)

- **Inputs:** GeoDAWN RTP magnetic grid, flight-line CSV/shapefile and processing metadata if the release supplies the needed fields, plus a 3DEP terrain model for registration. Do not invent per-cell aircraft height from terrain alone.
- **Physical signature:** a magnetic edge that remains coherent after separating flight-line/tie-line direction and documented leveling effects, with independent geological or gravity support.
- **Why it could find an uncatalogued fault:** a basement contact or offset under alluvium may remain in the magnetic field but be obscured or fragmented by acquisition-direction artifacts.
- **Named non-fault mimic:** tie-line microleveling or an intrusive/lithologic contact. Directional filtering alone is not enough; require corroboration and report the effective source resolution.
- **Difference from implemented work:** earlier lanes apply transforms to gridded RTP/TMI channels, but the audit did not find an implemented transform using flight-line geometry and verified sensor-clearance metadata together.
- **Official source and availability check:** the [USGS GeoDAWN data release](https://www.usgs.gov/data/geodawn-airborne-magnetic-and-radiometric-surveys-northwestern-great-basin-nevada-and) is described in the local source ledger as listing GeoTIFF grids, line CSVs, flight-path shapefiles and processing reports. No line-level file is in the local manifest or checkout, and the official page was not refreshed this session. The local source ledger supports 400 m **Area 2 line spacing** and 4,000 m tie-line spacing; it does not establish a 400 m flight/drape height or guarantee a height field in the line file. Confirm exact metadata before implementation.
